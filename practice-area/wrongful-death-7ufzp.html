What Is a Wrongful Death Claim?

A wrongful death claim is a civil action brought when a person dies as a result of the negligent, reckless, or intentional conduct of another. It is separate from any criminal case that may arise from the same event. A wrongful death claim does not seek to punish through imprisonment, but rather to compensate surviving family members for the harm the death has caused them. Because the claim belongs to the survivors, it focuses on their losses, both financial and personal.

Common Causes of Wrongful Death

Wrongful death claims can arise from many types of incidents, including:

  • Motor vehicle collisions, including car, truck, motorcycle, pedestrian, and bicycle accidents.
  • Medical negligence, when a preventable error leads to a patient's death.
  • Dangerous or defective products.
  • Unsafe property conditions, including inadequate security.
  • Nursing home neglect and elder abuse.
  • Workplace and construction accidents.
  • Intentional acts of violence.

Who Can File a Wrongful Death Claim in California

California law specifies who may bring a wrongful death claim. Generally, this includes the deceased person's surviving spouse or domestic partner, children, and, if there are no surviving children, other close relatives who would inherit under state law. In some cases, individuals who were financially dependent on the deceased may also have a right to recover. Determining who is entitled to bring a claim can be complex, and our firm helps families understand their rights and navigate this process during a difficult time.

Damages Available to Surviving Family Members

Wrongful death damages are intended to compensate survivors for both economic and non-economic losses. These may include:

  • Financial support the deceased would have provided, including income and benefits.
  • The value of household services the deceased provided.
  • Funeral and burial expenses.
  • Loss of the deceased's love, companionship, comfort, and guidance.
  • Loss of consortium for a surviving spouse or partner.

In addition, a related action known as a survival action may allow the deceased person's estate to recover certain losses the deceased experienced before death, such as medical expenses and, in some cases, other damages. The value of a wrongful death claim depends on many factors, and prior results do not guarantee a similar outcome.

Proving a Wrongful Death Claim

To succeed in a wrongful death claim, the family generally must establish that another party owed a duty of care to the deceased, that the party breached that duty through negligent or wrongful conduct, and that the breach caused the death and resulting losses. This often requires careful investigation, medical and other expert testimony, and a thorough accounting of the family's losses. Our firm handles this work so that grieving families can focus on one another.

Time Limits for Wrongful Death Claims

In California, a wrongful death lawsuit generally must be filed within two years of the date of death. When a public entity may be responsible, a formal claim usually must be presented within six months. There can be exceptions and nuances depending on the circumstances, so it is important to consult an attorney as soon as possible to protect the family's rights.

Understanding Economic and Non-Economic Losses

Wrongful death claims recognize that a family loses far more than a source of income when a loved one dies. Economic losses include the financial support the person would have provided over their lifetime, the value of the services they contributed to the household, and expenses such as funeral and burial costs. Calculating these losses often requires a careful analysis of the person's earnings, benefits, and life expectancy, and it may involve professionals who can help project what the family has lost.

Non-economic losses reflect the deeply personal harm of losing a loved one, including the loss of their love, companionship, comfort, guidance, and, for a spouse or partner, the loss of the marital relationship. These losses are real, even though they cannot be measured with a receipt. Presenting them in a way that honors the person who was lost, while meeting the requirements of the law, is a task our firm approaches with sensitivity and care.

The Survival Action and How It Differs

In addition to a wrongful death claim, the law recognizes a related action known as a survival action. While a wrongful death claim compensates surviving family members for their own losses, a survival action allows the deceased person's estate to recover certain losses that the deceased personally suffered between the time of injury and the time of death. These two actions are distinct but often pursued together, and understanding the difference is important to ensuring that a family recovers everything the law allows.

A survival action may include the medical expenses the deceased incurred before death and, depending on the circumstances, other losses the deceased experienced. Because the survival action belongs to the estate rather than to individual family members, it involves different considerations, including how any recovery is handled through the estate. Coordinating a wrongful death claim and a survival action requires care, and pursuing both together helps ensure that neither the family's losses nor the estate's losses are overlooked. Our firm evaluates whether both actions apply in a given case and pursues each avenue of recovery available. We handle the legal complexities so that the family can focus on one another, and we approach every aspect of these cases with the sensitivity that the loss of a loved one deserves. No legal action can undo such a loss, but a thorough and well-prepared claim can provide meaningful financial support and a measure of justice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a wrongful death claim the same as a criminal case?

No. A criminal case is brought by the government and can result in penalties like imprisonment. A wrongful death claim is a civil action brought by the family to recover compensation, and it can proceed regardless of whether criminal charges are filed.

Who receives the compensation?

Compensation generally goes to the surviving family members entitled to bring the claim, such as a spouse, domestic partner, or children, according to California law.

How long do we have to file?

Generally two years from the date of death, but shorter deadlines apply when a public entity is involved. It is best to seek advice promptly.

What if we are not sure who was responsible?

We investigate the circumstances of the death to identify every party who may bear responsibility.

Will pursuing a claim require our family to go to court?

Many wrongful death cases resolve through negotiated settlements without a trial. We prepare every case thoroughly and are ready to go to court when necessary, but we also work to spare families unnecessary burden where a fair resolution can be reached.

What if more than one family member wants to bring a claim?

California law addresses how a wrongful death claim proceeds when multiple family members are involved. Generally the claim is brought together on behalf of those entitled to recover, and we help families navigate this process.

How is the value of a wrongful death claim determined?

Value depends on many factors, including the financial support the deceased would have provided, the services they contributed, and the personal losses the family has suffered. Each case is unique, and prior results do not guarantee a similar outcome.

We are still grieving. Is it too early to speak with a lawyer?

We understand, and there is no pressure. A confidential consultation simply allows you to understand your rights and the deadlines that may apply. You can take the time you need while preserving your options.
