Representing Injured Construction Workers

Construction is essential work, and it is also among the most dangerous. Workers face heights, heavy machinery, power tools, and countless hazards every day. When negligence, unsafe conditions, or inadequate safety precautions cause an injury, the results can be catastrophic. At Young Law Group, we advocate for construction workers across California who have been hurt on the job, working to secure the compensation they need to recover and support their families.

Construction site injury cases can be legally complex. They often involve multiple parties, overlapping safety regulations, and questions about how the workers' compensation system interacts with other potential claims. Our firm helps injured workers understand their rights and pursue every avenue of recovery available to them.

Common Causes of Construction Accidents

Many construction injuries are preventable and result from a failure to follow safety practices. Common causes include:

  • Falls from heights, including from scaffolding, ladders, roofs, and unprotected edges.
  • Being struck by objects, such as falling tools, materials, or debris.
  • Caught-in or caught-between accidents, including trench collapses and machinery entanglement.
  • Electrocution from contact with live wires or faulty equipment.
  • Machinery and equipment accidents, including defective or improperly maintained equipment.
  • Vehicle and heavy equipment accidents on and around the site.
  • Exposure to hazardous substances.
  • Fires and explosions.

Serious Injuries on Construction Sites

The forces involved in construction accidents can cause severe, life-altering injuries, including:

  • Traumatic brain injuries.
  • Spinal cord injuries and paralysis.
  • Broken bones and crush injuries.
  • Amputations.
  • Burns and electrical injuries.
  • Vision and hearing loss.
  • Injuries resulting in permanent disability.

These injuries often require extensive medical care, long rehabilitation, and sometimes prevent a worker from returning to their trade. A thorough claim accounts for the full extent of these losses, including future needs.

Workers' Compensation and Third-Party Claims

Many injured construction workers are entitled to workers' compensation benefits, which can provide medical care and a portion of lost wages regardless of fault. However, workers' compensation does not always fully compensate a worker for the harm they have suffered, and it generally does not include compensation for pain and suffering. In many cases, an injured worker may also have a separate claim against a party other than their employer, known as a third-party claim.

Third-party claims can arise when someone other than the worker's employer contributed to the injury. Potentially responsible parties may include general contractors, subcontractors, property owners, equipment manufacturers, and others whose negligence or defective products caused harm. These claims can provide compensation beyond what workers' compensation offers. Determining whether a third-party claim exists requires a careful review of who was involved and what caused the accident.

Safety Regulations and Responsibility

Construction sites are governed by safety regulations designed to protect workers. When those responsible for a site fail to follow required safety practices and a worker is injured as a result, that failure can be important evidence of responsibility. Common issues include inadequate fall protection, unsafe scaffolding, lack of proper training, failure to guard machinery, and failure to address known hazards. Our firm examines whether safety obligations were met and how any failures contributed to a worker's injury.

Compensation for Injured Workers

Depending on the claims available, an injured construction worker may be able to recover:

  • Medical expenses for past and future treatment.
  • Lost wages and diminished earning capacity.
  • Costs of rehabilitation and vocational retraining.
  • Pain, suffering, and emotional distress, through a third-party claim.
  • Compensation for permanent disability.

The compensation available depends on the specific facts and the claims that apply, and prior results do not guarantee a similar outcome.

Time Limits That Apply

Construction injury claims are subject to deadlines that depend on the type of claim. Workers' compensation claims have their own reporting and filing requirements, and third-party personal injury claims are generally subject to a two-year statute of limitations. Because prompt action helps preserve evidence and protect your rights, it is important to consult an attorney as soon as possible after an injury.

Steps To Take After a Construction Injury

  • Seek medical attention right away and report the injury to your employer.
  • Document the scene, the equipment involved, and your injuries if you are able.
  • Identify witnesses and preserve their contact information.
  • Keep records of your medical treatment and expenses.
  • Avoid giving detailed statements to insurers before speaking with an attorney.
  • Consult an attorney to identify all available claims.

Why Third-Party Claims Matter

For many injured construction workers, the difference between an adequate recovery and a full one comes down to whether a third-party claim exists. Workers' compensation provides important benefits, but it does not compensate a worker for pain and suffering, and it may not fully replace lost earning capacity for a seriously injured worker. A third-party claim, brought against a party other than the employer whose negligence or defective product contributed to the injury, can provide compensation for these additional losses.

Construction sites typically involve many different companies and individuals, including general contractors, subcontractors, property owners, equipment suppliers, and manufacturers. When one of these parties fails to follow safety requirements or provides defective equipment, an injured worker may have a claim against them in addition to workers' compensation. Determining whether such a claim exists requires a careful review of who was present, what caused the accident, and how responsibility is shared, which is a core part of what our firm does in these cases.

How the Two Systems Work Together

One of the most important things an injured construction worker can understand is how workers' compensation and a third-party claim can work together. Workers' compensation is generally available regardless of who was at fault and provides medical care and a portion of lost wages relatively quickly. It is often the first source of support after an injury. However, it does not compensate a worker for pain and suffering, and its wage benefits may fall short of a seriously injured worker's actual losses.

A third-party claim can fill that gap. When a party other than the employer, such as a general contractor, subcontractor, property owner, or equipment manufacturer, contributed to the injury, that party can be pursued for the full range of damages, including pain and suffering and the complete extent of lost earning capacity. These two paths are not mutually exclusive, and pursuing both can be important to a full recovery. There are rules about how the two interact, including how a workers' compensation insurer may seek reimbursement from a third-party recovery, and navigating these rules is part of what an experienced attorney does. We help injured workers understand the full picture so they can make informed decisions and pursue every source of compensation available to them and their families.

Frequently Asked Questions

I am receiving workers' compensation. Can I still bring another claim?

Possibly. If a party other than your employer contributed to your injury, you may have a third-party claim in addition to workers' compensation.

What is the difference between the two?

Workers' compensation provides certain benefits regardless of fault but does not include pain and suffering. A third-party claim can provide broader compensation when another party is responsible.

Who might be responsible besides my employer?

General contractors, subcontractors, property owners, and equipment manufacturers are among the parties who may bear responsibility, depending on the facts.

How soon should I act?

As soon as possible. Prompt action helps preserve evidence and protects your rights under the applicable deadlines.

Will filing a claim affect my job?

Workers have legal protections, and you should not be retaliated against for pursuing the benefits and compensation you are entitled to. If you have concerns about your employment, we can discuss them with you.

What if I am an undocumented worker?

Injured workers may have rights regardless of immigration status. We encourage anyone injured on a construction site to seek advice about their options.

How quickly do I need to act after a construction injury?

As soon as possible. Reporting requirements for workers' compensation and deadlines for third-party claims both make prompt action important, and early investigation helps preserve evidence before a site changes.

What will it cost to hire your firm?

We handle third-party construction injury claims on a contingency basis, meaning there are no upfront costs and no attorney fees unless we recover for you.
